The most powerful use of the CPT is the most direct one: because a cone is, in effect, a miniature pile, its measurements can feed foundation design with minimal intermediate steps.

## Axial pile capacity

CPT-based methods dominate modern driven-pile design, particularly offshore:

* The four "CPT methods" formalised in API/ISO practice — **ICP-05** (Imperial College), **UWA-05**, **Fugro-05**, and **NGI-05** — estimate shaft friction and end bearing directly from q<sub>c</sub>/q<sub>t</sub> profiles, accounting for friction fatigue, soil displacement, and interface friction.
* The **Unified CPT-based method** (Lehane and co-workers, 2020s) consolidated these into a single recommended approach for sands and clays, now embedded in API RP 2GEO / ISO 19901-4 practice.

## Shallow foundations

Settlement-based design of footings on sand from CPT profiles goes back to Schmertmann's strain-influence method (1970s), with elastic-modulus correlations from q<sub>c</sub>; bearing capacity checks follow classical theory with CPT-derived strength parameters.

## The frontier: lateral response of monopiles

Extending CPT-based design from axial capacity to the **lateral response of large-diameter monopiles** is an active research front — and a central theme of my own work:

* In the [MIDAS](/portfolio/midas/) project we evaluated CPT-based p–y models against centrifuge tests, including in-flight CPTs performed in the same samples as the pile tests — see [Wang et al. (2022), CPT'22](/publication/2022-06-02-centrifuge-study-on-the-cpt-based-p-y).
* Building on that, we developed an **SCPT-based monotonic soil reaction model** for monopiles in sand, calibrated against centrifuge tests and validated against field data — see [Pisanò et al. (2026, under review)](/publication/2026-01-02-an-scpt-based-monotonic-soil-reaction-model-for).
* My earlier field and numerical work at UWA produced a **CPT-based approach for the lateral capacity of rigid piles in sand** — see [Wang et al. (2020), Géotechnique Letters](/publication/2020-09-01-a-simple-approach-for-predicting-the-ultimate-lateral).

The appeal is the same as for axial design: the CPT feels the same soil the pile will, at prototype stress levels, with no laboratory-to-field translation. The challenge is that lateral loading mobilises the soil differently than a penetrating cone — which is exactly where physical modelling and instrumented pile tests earn their keep.
